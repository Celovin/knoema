# Gunness 1900s Historical Profile Research

## Overview

Gunness 1900s is a Tier 2 historical canonical profile for Belle Gunness, born Brynhild Paulsdatter Storset or Storseth (1859-disappeared 1908). The case is public-domain and pre-1930, but it remains historically disputed because Gunness' death was uncertain and some claims rest on newspaper reporting, local memory, and later reconstructions. The replay profile should focus on confidence-building, financial motive, correspondence, and rural-to-commercial trust translation. It should not repeat inflated victim counts as fact, should not name victims in the viewer, and should not encode real deception scripts. The strongest academic support comes from broader female serial homicide literature, not case-specific peer-reviewed work, so the YAML must label its case claims as source-limited.

## Primary sources

Primary sources include contemporary La Porte-area newspaper coverage, court reporting around Ray Lamphere, local archival materials, and photographic records such as the Library of Congress Belle Gunness image [C1]. Later books report use of letters and local witness interviews, but those are mediated secondary sources [C2, C3]. Because the case involved fire, contested identification, and post-event legend, the profile should use primary material only for broad historical context: farm setting, correspondence with suitors, financial motive allegations, and disputed disappearance. It should not encode contested victim counts or detailed harm methods.

## Academic secondary sources

Harrison and colleagues provide peer-reviewed empirical context on female serial killers in the United States, including common motives and victim relationships across a historical sample [C4]. Eriksson and colleagues provide broader peer-reviewed context on serious female homicide offenders and criminal careers [C5]. Schechter's Hell's Princess is a modern historical secondary for the Gunness case [C2]. De la Torre's The Truth about Belle Gunness is an older narrative source with witness-history value but should be treated cautiously [C3]. Cohen and Felson support opportunity modeling [C6]. Gangnam and Seoul CPTED sources support urban translation [C7, C8]. This mix is adequate only for a cautious educational profile, not for strong case-specific behavioral claims.

## Behavioral analysis

### Victim profile

The profile should not encode named victims or demographic targeting. Historical accounts often describe adult male suitors, financial assets, and correspondence-based trust [C2, C3]. Broader female serial homicide literature identifies financial gain as a common motive in the studied U.S. female serial killer sample [C4]. In the replay, the safe target criterion is "trust-building interaction with low verification support." The profile must avoid victim-blaming and must not imply that loneliness, migration, or wealth makes a person responsible for victimization.

### Modus operandi

MO rules should be abstract: establish trust cue, invite commitment, increase isolation, abort when verification appears, and seek financial advantage. The rules must not include letters, exact wording, payment instructions, or harm methods. The point is to show how social deception can be modeled in agent memory without operationalizing it. The profile should be visually distinct from street-convergence archetypes.

### Signature elements

No signature elements should be encoded. The case is already sensationalized; the replay should reduce, not increase, myth. The YAML should include a myth-caution note and a rule that the profile cannot display victim names or disputed counts.

### Geographic behavior

Historically, the Gunness case is farm- and correspondence-centered rather than street-geographic. The contemporary grid translation should therefore use commercial trust cells and transit edges, not alley hunting. The mobility radius can be low, with the profile staying near a "trusted venue" anchor. Rossmo-style geographic concepts are used only as general anchor vocabulary [C9].

### Temporal patterns

The profile should operate over relationship-building ticks rather than exact times. Since the replay lasts only 30 simulated minutes, it compresses a long historical process into symbolic actions: contact, trust cue, verification challenge, disengagement. It should not imply that the historical case unfolded on such a timescale.

### Victim-offender interaction dynamics

Interaction should be rendered as memory labels: "trust cue offered," "verification requested," "guardian present," and "interaction aborted." The viewer should never show a raw letter or script. This approach is useful for teaching social guardianship and verification support.

## Geographic profiling data

No case-specific geographic profile is encoded. The historical case involved cross-place correspondence and a rural property; the replay uses a synthetic city grid for demonstration. Anchor language comes from geographic profiling literature [C9], but the profile's 1-2 anchor points and commercial-cell preference are interface parameters. The report should state that Gunness-specific academic sources are thinner than for Tier 1 typologies and that the profile is intentionally conservative.

## Contemporary Seoul translation

The Gunness translation maps rural correspondence and suitor trust to modern commercial and service interactions. Gangnam is used only because it is the replay's shared grid and has literature support as a planned commercial district [C7]. Seoul CPTED research supports the broader idea that guardianship and environmental cues shape safety perception and behavior [C8]. For this profile, "guardian" should include a peer verifier, clerk, or institutional warning, not only police. The overlay should demonstrate that verification interrupts deception.

## Ethical considerations

The Gunness case has uncertain death status and disputed numbers. The profile must not state a body count as fact. It should say "canonical historical case, disputed details." Because the figure disappeared in 1908 and is permitted by the Tier 2 list, Codex may create the profile, but only with restrained, source-limited claims. No living relatives, contemporary analogues, or Korean cases should be invoked. The ethical aim is prevention education and source criticism.

Implementation should make the profile slower and more trust-oriented than the street-convergence profiles. The YAML can express a sequence of symbolic states: establish contact, request commitment, encounter verification, disengage, and reset. These states are safe because they do not reproduce letters, exact promises, payment requests, or any operational script. The viewer should display them as memory labels rather than dialogue. This keeps the overlay useful for discussing social deception while preventing misuse.

The profile must also surface thin-source caution. Compared with FBI typology or geographic profiling literature, Gunness has less case-specific peer-reviewed criminology. The research note therefore uses broader female serial homicide studies to support general motive and relationship cautions, while case-specific details come from historical books and primary archives. The YAML should not hide that limitation. A "source-limited historical profile" label is appropriate and should be visible in the profile panel.

For the Seoul grid, the historical farm and correspondence setting becomes a commercial trust analogy. That translation is intentionally artificial. It allows a non-violent, non-graphic lesson about verification guardianship: peer checks, institutional warnings, and delayed commitment can interrupt deception. The profile should not imply that women, immigrants, rural residents, or any contemporary social group is suspicious. It should discuss behavior states, not identities.

The profile should avoid unresolved-death speculation. Whether Gunness died in 1908 or escaped is not necessary for the replay. The display name can identify the historical case, but the behavior rules should not depend on the disappearance theory. The safest classroom use is to ask how uncertain sources affect profile construction and why a simulation should mark uncertainty instead of converting it into confident behavior.

The profile should also make clear that "financial motive" is a historical-literature abstraction, not a moral claim about poverty, migration, gender, or relationship status. Its rules should be written in terms of verification and trust, because those are prevention-relevant. This keeps the replay focused on social guardianship and memory modeling while reducing the risk of stereotyping.

The safer product lesson is that social verification can be simulated without reproducing a harmful script. A profile row such as "peer check interrupts commitment" is useful because it shows prevention logic. A row that imitates historical correspondence would be unsafe and unnecessary. The overlay should therefore use short, neutral state labels and leave detailed historical debate in the research note.

That separation between teaching state and historical narrative is mandatory.
